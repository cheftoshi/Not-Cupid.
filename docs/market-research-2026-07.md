## 1. Landscape map (by model)

**A. Curated-IRL / ritual (the hot category — where the money + momentum is)**
- **Timeleft** — weekly algorithm-matched dinners of ~5 strangers, Wednesdays 7pm, at partner restaurants ("5 strangers, 3 hours"). **~150k dinner participants/month, 200+ cities, ~€18M ARR ~20 months post-launch.** The scale benchmark for ritual-based curated connection. Monetizes via subscription to the ritual.
- **222** — AI personality-test-matched GROUP outings (dinners, comedy) at local venues; **no profiles, no DMs, no swiping.** **Raised a $10.1M Series A (Dec 2025, Upfront Ventures / Mark Suster).** ~$22.22 curation fee / subscription. Explicitly targets Gen Z fleeing dating apps.
- **Clyx** — Gen Z-founded, **raised $14M (Sept 2025)**; standout NEW mechanic = **"Programs": recurring event series that put the SAME group together repeatedly.** (The most important learning in this whole report — see §3.)
- **Les Amís** — AI-matched friendship for women/trans/LGBTQ+, weekly MONDAY matching, city-priced membership ($70 NYC), **expanding to Boston.** A direct women-first weekly-ritual competitor entering NotCupid's home market.
- Also active per TechCrunch's late-2025 friend-competitor checklist: **Clockout, Clyx, Les Amís, Timeleft, 222.**

**B. Feed / swipe (structurally declining)**
- **Tinder** −3% rev / −7% payers YoY (Q3-25); Match flagged a Tinder overhaul for spring 2026. **Bumble** first-ever revenue decline (−9.6% to $782M; users 42M→35M; subs 2.8M→2.4M).
- **Keeny** (a founder-named app — RESOLVED) — a "meet IRL" dating app built on a **live FEED of posts** instead of profile stacks; no swiping, verified-only. I.e. it fights swipe-fatigue with a feed — a different bet than curation.

**C. Intent / personality-first (the winner within dating)**
- **Hinge** — +27% direct rev to $184.7M, payers +17% to 1.87M (Q3-25). Grew ~25% in 2025 while the overall dating market *declined ~2% to $6B* (its first-ever annual decline). Proof: it's the swipe/engagement model that's broken, not online dating.

**D. Friendship-matching (a real but SMALL paid market)**
- The whole US paid-friendship category: ~$16M consumer spend + ~4.3M downloads in 2025 across a dozen+ apps. **Bumble BFF** relaunched Sept-2025 pivoting from 1:1 swiping → **friend GROUPS + community** (Groups tab, hangout planning, in-app calendar), built on **Geneva** (acquired 2024) — but **Geneva generated $0 revenue and its standalone app is being shut down.** BFF/Bizz have never matched the core app's traction.
- Common BFF complaints (teardowns): matching feels random / no shared interests on free tier; 24-hour message timers create pressure.

**E. Event platforms / community-directory (adjacent, not the core game)**
- Partiful, Posh, Meetup, Geneva — event/community tooling. Geneva's $0-revenue shutdown is the cautionary tale: community directories don't monetize directly.

## 2. Differentiation — NotCupid's defensible wedge

**Genuinely unique (nobody else combines these):**
1. **Dual-line from ONE psychological profile** — dating (Love) AND friendship (Friend) off a single quiz. Timeleft/222/Clyx are social/friend-only; Hinge is dating-only; Bumble's modes are bolted on and BFF is failing. "One authentic profile, two ways to connect" is a story no competitor can tell.
2. **Roster-CHOOSE for dating** — a curated set you pick from (no swipe, no browse, no feed). Closest cousin is Hinge Standouts, but more constrained + anti-choice-overload. On-trend with the curation shift.
3. **Ritual applied to the ROSTER, not the event** — Timeleft/222's ritual is the dinner; NotCupid's is the weekly DROP of *people to choose from*. Roster-first, async, digital-entry — lower commitment than "show up to dinner with 4 strangers sight-unseen."

**Overlapping / at-risk (don't fight on these):**
- The **Scene / City Pulse** overlaps Partiful/Posh/Meetup/Geneva — and Geneva just proved directories don't monetize. This is the weakest, most-cloned surface.
- The broad **Friend Line** sits in a small paid market; the category's money is in the ritual + IRL outcome, not match volume.

**The strategic tension the data surfaces:** the winners (Timeleft, 222, Clyx) all converge on **repeated, in-person, small-group, ritual-cadence** connection. NotCupid HAS the ritual (weekly drop) + small-group (packs/crews) but is **weakest exactly where the category is heading: repeated same-group meeting + a real IRL bridge.**

## 3. PMF recommendations (ranked)

**DOUBLE DOWN**
1. **The dual-line + roster-choose** — the actual moat and the on-trend bet (curation over swipe/feed). Lead positioning with it.
2. **The weekly drop as a ritual SUBSCRIPTION** — Timeleft proves ritual-cadence monetizes (€18M). Make **All-Access ($3.99/mo) the "never wait / never miss" ritual sub** the durable revenue; the $0.99 packs are impulse garnish. (NotCupid is *underpriced* vs the category — 222 charges $22.22, Les Amís $70 — room to test a higher experience/priority tier later.)
3. **Women-first / safe-space friend spaces** — Les Amís is entering Boston with precisely this. NotCupid already has the safety guardrails + own-gender-only spaces. **Race them in the home market.**

**ADD — new mechanics (the founder wants NEW, not recycled icebreakers):**
1. **★ Recurring crews / "keep this crew going" (Clyx-Programs-inspired) — the #1 add.** Friendship forms through *repetition with the same people*, not one-shot meetings — and Clyx's $14M is the market validating it. Let a crew opt into a recurring thing (weekly coffee, monthly hike); the crew *levels up / unlocks* by actually re-meeting. This is the earlier "proof-of-hang / crew-levels" idea, now externally confirmed as the category's direction. It also keeps the app a *connection* app — it rewards real meeting, not screen time.
2. **A crew-native IRL bridge (Timeleft's dinner, but yours).** The money + stickiness is IRL — but don't out-Partiful Partiful. Instead: turn a pack/crew into ONE algo-suggested low-stakes meet (the app picks a time + public place in the city), one-tap "I'm in." Opt-in, crew-scoped — not a general events platform.
3. **Curation-as-trust for Gen Z.** 222's pitch is "curated, no profiles to perform for." NotCupid's roster-choose already has this DNA — brand it: *"curated, not a marketplace."*

**AVOID**
1. **Don't scale the Scene/City Pulse into a general event platform or Discord directory** — that's Partiful/Meetup/Geneva's turf, and Geneva ($0 revenue, shut down) proves directories don't pay. Keep the Scene a thin acquisition + crew-activation layer.
2. **Don't chase friend-match VOLUME** — small paid market ($16M total). Value = ritual + IRL outcome, not match count.
3. **Don't build games-for-games'-sake.** The bonding layer should be *real repeated meeting* (mechanic #1), not in-app minigames.

## 4. Positioning / design implications ("shine in every way," stay a CONNECTION app)

- **Lead with the anti-app stance.** Swipe/feed fatigue is driving the whole shift (Tinder/Bumble down, Hinge/222/Timeleft up). NotCupid's no-swipe / no-browse / no-feed is dead-on-trend — say it louder, everywhere.
- **Own the cadence.** Timeleft owns Wednesday dinners; NotCupid should own *"Thursdays, your people drop."* Ritual = brand.
- **Tell the dual-line story** — "one profile, two ways to connect" — the one thing no competitor can copy without a rebuild.
- **Every mechanic must end in a real conversation or a real meeting.** That's the guardrail against becoming a feed or a game. The recurring-crew mechanic is the purest expression: it rewards showing up, not logging in.
- **Design for curation, not marketplace.** Fewer, better, chosen-for-you. The roster and the pack already embody this — the Scene should feel curated too, not infinite.
